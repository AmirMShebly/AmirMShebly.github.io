PAMR: Precedence-Aware Mode Repair for Multimodal Trajectory Forecasting

Amirreza M. Shebly, Ehsan Garaaghaji, Doruk Oner

IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV), 2027 Accepted

Abstract

Multimodal trajectory forecasting models generate multiple future hypotheses and are typically evaluated through geometric displacement or endpoint errors. In interactive driving, however, an important source of uncertainty is ordinal: which agent enters a shared conflict zone first. We formalize this uncertainty as a precedence relation over agent–zone entry events and introduce three diagnostics that make it measurable: precedence coverage, order-collapse rate, and bad-winner selection. Across four widely used datasets and multiple forecasting model families, we find that learned K-mode predictors fail in all three aspects. First, they often fail to include the ground-truth precedence relation among their predicted modes. Second, their modes frequently collapse to redundant interaction orders despite being geometrically diverse. Third, even when a correct-order mode is present, the selected mode can still realize the wrong precedence. We propose PAMR, a Precedence-Aware Mode Repair pipeline that operates post-hoc on frozen multimodal predictors. PAMR has two stages. PAMR-Warp re-times base modes via a monotonic time-warp to generate precedence-targeted candidates. PAMR-Select then estimates the precedence from agent context and selects an order-consistent mode from the augmented pool of base and refined candidates. Across all four datasets, PAMR improves precedence coverage, lowers order-collapse rate, and reduces bad-winner selection while preserving geometric accuracy.

Direction-Aware Kinematics-Conditioned Graph Hypernetwork for Human Trajectory Prediction

Amirreza M. Shebly, Mohammad Sarhangzadeh, Doruk Oner

Asian Conference on Computer Vision (ACCV), 2026 — Oral Presentation Accepted

Paper (OpenReview) ↗

Abstract

Accurate human trajectory prediction requires modeling not only where pedestrians are, but also how they are moving relative to one another. Existing graph-based methods often rely on geometric proximity or learned attention, which can produce interaction structures weakly tied to physical motion cues. We propose a kinematics-conditioned graph hypernetwork for trajectory prediction, where inter-agent messages are generated from relative motion features such as velocity, alignment, directionality, and time-to-collision. This allows the model to capture anisotropic and context-dependent interactions while maintaining sparse, motion-grounded connectivity. The proposed framework supports both deterministic and probabilistic prediction and reduces unnecessary message passing without sacrificing accuracy. Experiments on widely used trajectory prediction benchmarks show that our approach improves over representative graph-based baselines and remains competitive with stronger recent attention- and transformer-style models, while offering stable sparsification and lower computational overhead.

Stochastic Nonlinearities Improve Uncertainty Estimation

Mohammad Sarhangzadeh, Amirreza M. Shebly, Doruk Oner

British Machine Vision Conference (BMVC), 2026 Accepted

Project page ↗

Abstract

Uncertainty estimation is crucial for vision tasks involving thin structures, ambiguous boundaries, and safety-sensitive decisions, such as aerial road extraction and medical image analysis. Many uncertainty estimation methods obtain predictive diversity through mechanisms that can suppress intermediate features, perturb the input, or require substantial additional computation. Stochastic activation functions, where network units randomly select between multiple nonlinearities during a forward pass, have previously been used to improve optimization behavior, mitigate limitations of fixed ReLU activations, and increase output diversity. In this work, we reframe stochastic activation switching as a lightweight mechanism for uncertainty estimation. The proposed method samples between nonlinear activation functions, inducing predictive diversity while keeping intermediate feature pathways active. This enables uncertainty estimation from a single trained model without the training cost of ensembles, task-specific uncertainty heads, or architectural redesign. We evaluate the approach across classification, semantic segmentation, and out-of-distribution detection using convolutional and transformer-based architectures on natural, aerial, and medical imagery. Across these settings, stochastic activation switching improves uncertainty quality while preserving competitive primary-task performance, showing that stochastic nonlinearities provide an effective and information-preserving source of predictive variation for visual recognition.
